SYNTHETIC CONCEPT · PUBLIC VISION

A human-guided compute constellation

An exploratory design for turning selected, repeatable agent workflows into evaluated programs that may run on smaller models.

We propose a second layer around agent-assisted work: identify a bounded pattern only after its owner opts in, curate a golden dataset, compare model and program candidates, and promote only with human approval. The goal is to test whether a narrower implementation can reduce compute cost while maintaining or improving delivery quality.

Proposed vision, not a deployed capability. The demo has no chat ingestion, active orchestration, job search, contract mining, sales action, provider calls, or trained models. All examples are invented.

Where the idea might apply

01 / INTERNAL WORK

Repeatable assistance

Opted-in support triage, document tagging, or meeting summaries could become narrowly scoped candidates.

02 / ORCHESTRATION

Agent coordination

A future system could study its own task-routing patterns under explicit review and authorization.

03 / EXTERNAL WORK

Opportunity research

Job search, contract research, and consulting proposals are possible research domains. This demo takes no external action.

Claims that all work can be reduced to compute are a broad thesis to examine, not an established property of people or organizations. Human judgment, relationships, legal duties, and physical work remain important constraints.

The controlled refinement loop

  1. Select and authorize: define one workflow, data boundary, intended use, owner, and stop conditions.
  2. Review and redact: remove sensitive material before examples enter a versioned dataset.
  3. Golden dataset: label representative cases, hold out a test split, record provenance, and refine labels through human adjudication.
  4. Choose candidates: compare a baseline with DSPy program configurations and smaller-model options using the same evaluation set.
  5. Evaluate: measure quality, cost, latency, failure modes, and uncertainty. A cheaper candidate should not advance if quality falls below an agreed threshold.
  6. Human approval: review evidence and risks before promoting a versioned program, dataset, and model selection.
  7. Rollback: monitor approved scope and restore the prior version when a gate or metric fails.

Testable hypotheses

H1: Some bounded workflows can use smaller models without unacceptable quality loss. H2: Adjudicated golden datasets improve repeatability and error discovery. H3: Versioned artifacts, human approvals, and rollback make experiments easier to govern. Each hypothesis needs real, permissioned evaluation before any savings or performance claim.

The numbers in the interactive UI are simulated. There are no measured cost reductions, production benchmarks, or customer outcomes here.
